# Social Impact Measurement & Evaluation Framework

## Problem Landscape in India

India has an estimated 26.8 million persons with disabilities (Census of India), with significant populations affected by speech, hearing, and cognitive communication challenges. Furthermore, each year over 1.8 million Indians experience strokes, frequently leading to acute or chronic expressive aphasia. 

Critical barriers in India include:
1. **Linguistic Pluralism**: Existing commercial AAC tools are predominantly Anglocentric and cost upwards of $200–$400, placing them out of reach of ordinary families and public hospitals.
2. **Connectivity Fragility**: Poor network connectivity in government hospital basements and rural transport nodes disrupts cloud-dependent tools.
3. **Complex Interfaces**: Many AAC boards require weeks of specialized speech-language pathologist (SLP) training before a patient can formulate simple emergency needs.

## Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

### 1. Inclusion & Access Metrics
- **Zero-Barrier Accessibility**: 0 ₹ cost, 0 accounts, 0 setup latency.
- **Linguistic Coverage**: Architectural inclusion of all 22 Eighth Schedule Indian languages, protecting indigenous and scheduled linguistic rights.
- **Offline Reliability**: 100% of curated emergency, clinical, and transit phrases available without an internet connection.

### 2. Clinical & Functional Communication Metrics
- **Reduction in Patient Distress**: Measured reduction in clinical anxiety scores during ICU stays or post-stroke rehabilitation when patients have an expressive outlet.
- **Diagnostic Clarification Speed**: Decreased time required for emergency room nurses and triage staff to identify patient pain location and immediate needs.
- **Public Service Independence**: Enables non-speaking individuals to navigate bus stops, ration offices, and banks without mandatory chaperoning.
